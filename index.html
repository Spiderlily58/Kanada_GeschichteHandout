01 · The beginning

Understanding Canada's Past

Canada's history is a story of cultural encounters, conflict, change and the gradual development of a nation. To understand modern Canada, we must look at the people and events that shaped its identity.

A country with many stories

Long before European explorers arrived, the land now known as Canada was home to diverse Indigenous peoples. They had their own languages, governments, traditions and trading networks.

European colonization began to transform the region from the 16th century onwards. French and British powers competed for control, leading to major political and cultural changes.

In 1867, Canada became a self-governing Dominion. Over time, it developed into the independent and diverse country we know today.

02 · Important dates

A Timeline of Canadian History

Click on each event to discover what happened and why it matters.

Before 1500
Indigenous civilizations

For thousands of years, First Nations, Inuit and Métis communities developed diverse cultures, political systems and trade networks across the land. Their societies were deeply connected to the environment.

1497
John Cabot reaches North America

The Italian explorer John Cabot, sailing for England, reached the Atlantic coast of North America. His voyage strengthened European interest in the region, although Indigenous peoples had lived there for thousands of years.

1534
Jacques Cartier explores the region

French explorer Jacques Cartier explored the Gulf of St. Lawrence and claimed parts of the region for France. This marked an important step in French colonial ambitions.

1608
Quebec City is founded

Samuel de Champlain founded Quebec City. It became a major centre of New France and an important base for the French fur trade and settlement.

1670
The Fur Trade & Hudson’s Bay Company

The King of England started a giant business called the Hudson’s Bay Company. This company got a monopoly (the exclusive right to do business) over a huge part of the land. This officially started the Fur Trade, where Europeans and Indigenous peoples traded furs for tools.

1763
Britain takes control of New France

After the Seven Years' War, France ceded most of its mainland North American territories to Britain in the Treaty of Paris. British rule transformed the political landscape of the region. The Royal Proclimation took place in the same year.

1774
The Quebec Act

The British were afraid the French settlers would rebel against them. So, they passed a law that allowed the French to keep their Catholic religion, their civil laws, and their language. This laid the foundation for Canada to become a bilingual country.

1812
The War of 1812

The United States launched a military invasion (attack) to conquer and take Canada. British soldiers, French settlers, and Indigenous tribes formed an unexpected alliance (partnership) and fought back together to successfully protect their land.

1867
Canadian Confederation

The British North America Act united Ontario, Quebec, Nova Scotia and New Brunswick into the Dominion of Canada. This is considered the birth of modern Canada.

1876
The Indian Act

The government created a very harsh law called the Indian Act. The goal was total assimilation (forcing a minority group to drop their own culture and adopt the lifestyle of the majority), which later led to the tragic residential school system.

1885
The completion of the railway

The Canadian Pacific Railway was completed, connecting eastern and western Canada. It encouraged migration and economic growth, but its construction also involved the exploitation of workers and the displacement of Indigenous communities.

1914–18
Canada in World War I

As part of the British Empire, Canada entered World War I in 1914. Canadian soldiers fought in major battles, including Vimy Ridge in 1917. The war strengthened Canada's international identity.

1939–45
Canada in World War II

Canada declared war on Germany in 1939. Canadian forces took part in the Battle of the Atlantic, the Italian Campaign and the D-Day landings. The war increased Canada's international influence.

1960s
The Quiet Revolution

Quebec experienced major social, political and economic changes. The province modernized rapidly, and questions about French language rights and Quebec's identity became increasingly important.

1982
The Constitution Act

Canada patriated its Constitution from Britain. The Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms became part of the Constitution, protecting fundamental rights and freedoms.

1999
Nunavut is created

Nunavut became Canada's newest territory. Its creation was an important step towards greater Inuit self-government and recognition of Indigenous rights.

Today
Modern Canada

Canada is an independent, bilingual and multicultural country. It continues to address important issues such as reconciliation with Indigenous peoples, immigration, climate change and national identity.

03 · Key topics

The Themes That Shaped Canada

Explore the most important themes in Canadian history. Open each box to learn more.

01 / PEOPLE

Indigenous Peoples

First Nations, Inuit and Métis communities have shaped Canada's history for thousands of years.

Explore this topic
Indigenous peoples are not one single group. They have distinct languages, cultures and traditions. Colonization brought land loss, discrimination and forced assimilation. Today, Indigenous rights and reconciliation remain central issues in Canada.
02 / COLONIZATION

European Colonization

French and British powers competed for territory, resources and influence in North America.

Explore this topic
France established settlements in New France, while Britain expanded its influence. The fur trade was a major part of the colonial economy. European colonization changed Indigenous societies and led to conflicts over land and power.
03 / POLITICS

Confederation

In 1867, several British colonies united to create the Dominion of Canada.

Explore this topic
Confederation began with four provinces: Ontario, Quebec, Nova Scotia and New Brunswick. The new country had its own federal government, while remaining connected to the British Empire. More provinces and territories joined later.
04 / WAR

The World Wars

Canada's participation in both World Wars helped shape its international identity.

Explore this topic
Canadian troops fought in Europe during both World Wars. Their contributions, sacrifices and experiences strengthened Canada's sense of nationhood and increased its international role.
05 / LANGUAGE

Quebec & Language

French and English are central to Canada's cultural and political identity.

Explore this topic
Canada has two official languages: English and French. Quebec is predominantly French-speaking. The Quiet Revolution of the 1960s brought major changes and strengthened the importance of French language rights.
06 / INJUSTICE

Residential Schools

A painful part of Canada's past was the forced assimilation of Indigenous children.

Explore this topic
For more than a century, Indigenous children were forcibly separated from their families and sent to residential schools. These institutions attempted to erase Indigenous languages and cultures. Their effects continue across generations. Canada is working towards truth, justice and reconciliation.
07 / SOCIETY

Multiculturalism

Canada is known for its cultural diversity and immigration history.

Explore this topic
People from many different countries and backgrounds have immigrated to Canada. In 1971, Canada adopted an official multiculturalism policy, recognizing cultural diversity as an important part of national identity.
08 / PRESENT

Canada Today

Modern Canada faces opportunities and challenges as a diverse democratic country.

Explore this topic
Canada is a federal parliamentary democracy and constitutional monarchy. Important contemporary topics include Indigenous reconciliation, immigration, climate policy, language rights and Canada's role in international affairs.
